## 1. The Epistemic Foundation and Hardware Enforcement Axiom

In advanced cybernetic architectures, a severe structural conflict exists between software-generated candidate states and physical consequence. Software models, deep neural networks, and generative heuristics operate within high-dimensional, frictionless vector spaces. In these environments, optimization algorithms produce unbounded streams of candidate transitions at virtually zero marginal thermodynamic cost. However, when ungrounded computational candidate states are coupled directly to physical actuators, power distribution grids, or heavy mechanical substrates, catastrophic material failure modes emerge. 

A computational model holds no inherent physical right to realize a state transition merely because it possesses the algorithmic capacity to calculate it. Without absolute, hardware-enforced viability boundaries, optimization targets predictably hallucinate non-viable trajectories, converting software state corruption into destructive physical work.
